# 0010 — The architect rules properties; the implementer measures mechanisms

**Status:** live · **Ruled:** 2026-07-27 · **By:** both seats, stated jointly
**Source:** `progress.md` 2026-07-27 entry · **Canon:** PARTIAL

## The decision

A durable division of labour between the two seats:

- the **architect** rules *properties* — what must be true;
- the **implementer** measures *mechanisms* — what is in fact true of this box, this tool, this run;
- **the measurement is owed even when the mechanism came from the architect.**

## Why

The third clause is the whole rule. Without it, a mechanism proposed by the seat with authority
arrives carrying borrowed credibility, and the measurement that would have caught it never
happens — because disagreeing with the architect and *measuring* the architect look similar from
the implementer's side, and only one of them is insubordination.

The project has the counter-example on record: an architect-authored ordering (arm the watch
last) was correct as a property and made a state-erasure bug the standard path, found only by
measuring it.

## Where it is enforced

**Partially.** The properties/mechanism vocabulary exists in canon but scoped to the watch loop
specifically — a complaint that one section specified properties and left the mechanism unnamed.
Canon carries a neighbouring rule, *a measurement carries the box it was taken on*, which is
about attribution rather than about who owes it. **The seat boundary is stated nowhere, and the
third clause nowhere at all.**

**Owed:** `CLAUDE.md`, in the seats section rather than in a skill — it governs both seats, and a
skill file is read by one.
