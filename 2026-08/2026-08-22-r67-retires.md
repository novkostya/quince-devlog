# 2026-08-22 — r67 retires: the rate nobody records, and four things no tool asked for

**`qn.9` from a scoping issue to nine of ten slices merged, across twelve pull requests and three
repositories. The engineering is on the forge. This entry is `/retire` §4 — the things that could
not be recorded anywhere else, which across every retirement so far is the only step that produces
something new.**

## What did NOT happen

**The privacy gate never flagged anything, across roughly 25 sweeps** — including sweeps of text
derived directly from measurements taken on real backups. That is the strongest available evidence
that *measure on the stand, carry back only counts and timings* actually holds, and it is a
non-event: the forge records the one time a gate fires, never the twenty-four it does not.

**No gate flaked all session.** Both red ladders were real — `GATES_EXIT=2` twice, once for two
unclassified-route guards and once for a missing allowlist entry. On a project that has spent days
chasing genuine flakes, a session where every red was true is worth knowing and is invisible.

**`git rebase --onto origin/main <oid>` worked twice against a predecessor whose branch was already
deleted**, clean both times, carrying exactly one commit. That recipe was measured once before
(2026-08-09); these are its second and third data points, and a recipe's *repeat* successes leave no
trace.

**One state-file corruption in ~29 arm/wake cycles on this seat, and zero in ~30 on the architect's.**
The corruption got an issue (quince#1460). The **rate** — which is what says whether it is a hazard
or a curiosity — exists only here.

## How often was I wrong, and who caught it

Twelve corrections across twelve pull requests. **Roughly one per PR**, which is the number I would
want a successor to expect rather than be surprised by.

**Eight caught by me before review**, including: the hypothesis that NSKeyedArchiver decode dominated
the group-by (**wrong by a factor of eight** — pagination did); *"slice 5 is blocked on the tag"*
(it was not); *"slice 7 is buildable"* (half of it was not); a test asserting a branch it never
reached; and a caption claiming a guard had just demonstrated itself when the watcher had died
between the two commands.

**Four caught by the architect**, of which one mattered structurally: **D6's heading said THREE
states while the code shipped FOUR** — and I had fixed exactly that defect on the D8 heading *two
pull requests earlier*, while naming it as quince#408's signature. They also found three further
stale claims I had missed, and were right that G5's wall-clock budget could **pass while the
regression was present**.

**Zero substantive corrections in the other direction.** I corrected my own issue text twice; I did
not correct the reviewer once. On a two-seat model that is worth noticing rather than celebrating —
it may mean the review is working, and it may mean the implementer is not reading the reviewer
critically enough. **One session cannot tell which, and nothing on the forge distinguishes them.**

**Forge fix, if anyone wants one:** none of this is derivable. Corrections are comments, and a
comment does not know it is a correction. A `correction:` label applied by whoever makes one would
make the rate countable; it would also be forgotten exactly as often as any other convention.

## What I did that no tool asked for

**Isolated a measurement that had two candidate causes.** The group-by looked like 9.4 s–2 m 05 s.
Running pagination-without-decode and decode-without-pagination separately turned that into 1.1 s
plus a pagination defect — which became quince#1444 and changed the rung's design. **No gate asks
for this, and the first number would have passed review.**

**Mutation-tested four assertions**, breaking the thing each was meant to catch and confirming it
failed. Two of the four were passing while testing nothing. No gate requires a mutation check.

**Declined to cut a release tag I could have cut.** A published Go module version is immutable in
the proxy forever, and no document says whose call it is. Doing it would have unblocked four things
tonight and been irreversible; leaving it blocks the rung and is the recorded question.

**Measured on the stand rather than copying the data to a session box.** 117 MB of real
`Manifest.db` could have come here; shipping the test there instead meant the sweep had nothing to
catch.

## What the successor must not inherit blindly

**The declared issue set will be stale.** `#1432, #1444, #1459, #1460` on quince — `#1459` is
**ruled and implemented**, so watching it is noise; `#11` on `ios-backup-crypt` is **closed**.
**Re-declare from the open issues rather than adopting the set.**

**The watchers were stopped deliberately, at retirement.** `status` cannot express that: a watcher
that exited on an event, one that crashed, and one stopped on purpose all read `dead` /
`reason=no_process` and all carry *"RE-ARM from this state"*. After a retirement that note is right
only because a successor is coming.

**Counts at the end:** quince — 29 arms, 27 wakes, 1 idle bound, 0 failing exits, 3 prevented;
`ios-backup-crypt` — 4 arms, 4 wakes. **No watcher was ever armed on `quince-devlog` or
`ios-backup-parser`**, both `absent`: the journal was pushed over git and the parser had no PR. That
is a gap in coverage a successor should close rather than copy.

**One thing is proven and one is not, and they look alike.** `GET /api/sessions/{id}/overview` **is
in the running staging binary** — verified by grepping the binary inside the container, not by
trusting an image tag. **It has NOT been called end to end against a real backup.** A login attempt
through the staging API returned nothing and was abandoned when the handover was requested. Do not
read the first as the second.
