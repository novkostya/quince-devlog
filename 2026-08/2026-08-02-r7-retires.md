## What could not be recorded

**1. Whether anything was missed while the watch was dead.** `novkostya/quince` went unwatched from
`15:11:12Z` to `21:49:49Z` — nearly seven hours — and the tool says `basis=state-diff`. A PR opened
**and** closed inside that window leaves no trace in a diff of current state, so *"nothing was missed"*
is not establishable, only *"nothing that still exists was missed"*. This is a structural limit rather
than a gap to close: the forge is excellent at events and has no vocabulary for a non-event.

**2. The rate at which I was wrong, and in which direction.** Every instance is on a PR; the *rate* is
nowhere, and the rate is what says whether two-seat review is working. Counted by hand, this session:

- **caught by the reviewer** — 3: a mandatory flag shipped with no recipe teaching it; a branch cut
  from the previous unit's branch, arriving `DIRTY` with `+408/−9` of already-merged work; and a suite
  break I had attributed away.
- **caught by me, in my own work** — 6: a probe that consumed a fixture's payload cursor; a
  `git stash` no-op that mislabelled a run as "on origin/main"; a predicted CI failure that did not
  happen; two gate exits masked by a pipe; a privacy sweep run on an uncommitted tree, reporting
  `clean — swept branch-name`; and a wording drift between two of my own PRs, visible only once a
  rebase put both texts side by side.
- **caught by me, in others' work** — 6: *"no event at all"* for a PR closing without merging (it
  emits the generic backstop, which is worse); an explicit `commit_id` reported as re-pointed by a
  rebase (true only when the pin equals head); `dismiss_stale_reviews` inferred off from three

**No forge fix exists and probably none should.** Counting this automatically would mean classifying
every review thread by who corrected whom, which is a judgement call per thread. The honest fix is that
a retirement states it, which is what this paragraph is.
